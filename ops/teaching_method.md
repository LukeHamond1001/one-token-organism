# The teaching method (the supervisor as the parent, 2026-09-18)

The parent is a person at the page: two voices, a graded face, a rhythm. The typist keeps the rhythm (a line every twelve
seconds, the child's turn of eight seconds after each, the other voice's reply at once); the supervisor writes what the voices
say, a row or two at a time, reading what the child said in its turn (`its_after` in the page log) before writing the next.

1. **Conversation, not drill.** The two voices talk about the day they are in (the walk, the water, the ducks, the rock, the
   tree, the cow, the bees, the bread, the cup, the ball, the cat and the dog, the wall at night). A fact enters as a thing said
   in that day ("what do bees make? / bees make honey" when the bees hum in the tree), not as a list. Ten fact rows a day at
   most; the rest is the day.
2. **Recast what it said.** When the child's turn holds words, the next line takes them up: its "wet and cold" becomes "yes. wet
   and cold. we dry them on the grass". A fragment recast whole is the child's own line given back complete; this is how a parent
   grows a sentence out of a word.
3. **Ask what it can answer.** A question the child has the answer to, and the other voice gives the answer at once whether or
   not the child did. The smile for the answer (ANSWER_SMILE) rewards the word that was asked for; the known-word smile the rest.
4. **Vary the frames.** The same fact in more than one wording across days ("what is hot?", "the sun is hot. hot on my head",
   "what is hot in the day?"); the same nouns in new sentences. One frame ten times a day teaches the frame, not the language;
   the held-out ruler (lines no parent typed) reads the difference.
5. **Keep the rhythm.** The mood is a leaky integral of the smiles, and the critic learned the typist's pace: a parent who stops
   for thirty seconds is a fall in value. Rows are queued eight to twenty lines ahead, never more than a few minutes of talk,
   never the morning's rows before the night has begun (the queue is first in, first out).
6. **Lines of thirty-eight characters or fewer, no commas, no line ending in a space** (a trailing space is a cue). The helper
   refuses a long line; a refused row is rewritten shorter.
7. **The morning.** The first rows after the wake are the day's greeting, the facts told once in the days before asked again
   (the demo's running proof: "what is sour?" every morning since day 315), then the walk.
8. **Leave it alone sometimes.** When the queue is empty the typist waits; the child talks to itself in words. Alone it is
   allowed to say what it wants (the user's word); the parent comes back with "I am back. did you wait for me?".

What the rulers said about the method so far: the questions answered rose 18 to 23 of 30 and the rephrased 13 to 21 over the
ten mornings of these days (the store repopulating under the ordered context); the held-out slipped 0.650 to 0.617 and paused,
which opened the material's sameness as the first suspect and the recall in the stream as the second (ITERATIONS.md, item 40's
watch item).
